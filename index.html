Case study: making Prey the go-to source on Chile's cybersecurity and data protection laws. I led Prey's Spanish content on Law 21.663 (cybersecurity framework) and Law 21.719 (personal data protection), written for three kinds of search: Google results (SEO), answer boxes (AEO) and AI assistants (GEO). The goal was for Prey to be the source people found when they asked what these laws mean for them. Today Google's AI Overviews cite Prey for "ley 21663 que es" and "ley 21719 que es", and ChatGPT has cited the same content. Results vary by query and day.

Project

Trip·3·o

An arcade color-mixing puzzle game of about 18,600 lines of React, built with Vite, PIXI.js (WebGL) and the Web Audio API, and deployed as a PWA. Its companion character uses procedural dialogue that simulates an AI.

Moved the renderer from CSS to WebGL, with a WebGL2 → WebGL1 → Canvas 2D fallback, and optimized it for mid-range phones (no per-frame allocations, cell diff rendering, LRU texture cache): from 24 to 53–58 FPS.

About

I started writing SEO content and buying guides in 2015, moved into iGaming content for Latin American markets, and since 2022 I've worked in cybersecurity and compliance. I've written about GDPR, HIPAA, FERPA, NIST and Chile's laws 21.719 (data protection) and 21.663 (cybersecurity framework), and I've worked with sales teams on custom compliance guides.

The part of the job I like most is taking something an expert says accurately but densely and finding the version a busy reader can use.

Outside work, I teach people in underserved communities in Venezuela how to spot scams and phishing, and I keep a photographic journal of the country.
